Northern Colorado City Comparison — Fort Collins, Loveland, Windsor & Greeley

Choosing between Northern Colorado’s core cities is rarely about a single number. Commute, schools, lot size, and how far your dollar goes all shift from one town to the next — and the I-25 corridor towns in between add a third set of trade-offs. This comparison, prepared by Schwartz and Associates (SAA Homes), Coldwell Banker Realty, walks through the differences so you can narrow the map before you start touring.

The four core markets at a glance

  • Fort Collins real estate agents — CSU, Old Town, and the Poudre River shape demand; close-in neighborhoods hold value while east and south build-out adds newer inventory.
  • Loveland CO homes for sale — the mid-corridor option between Fort Collins and Longmont, with mature east-side blocks and lake-centered recreation.
  • Windsor CO real estate — Weld RE-4 schools plus newer lakefront and golf-course communities pull families across the county line.
  • Greeley CO homes for sale — the most accessible price point in the region, with UNC and west-side growth anchoring steady demand.

The I-25 corridor & Boulder-county edge

  • Timnath CO new construction — master-planned communities set the upper end of the corridor curve.
  • Johnstown CO homes for sale — small-town pricing at the I-25 / US-34 crossroads with new phases coming online.
  • Berthoud CO real estate — limited inventory between Loveland and Longmont keeps competition steady.
  • Erie CO real estate agents — St. Vrain Valley schools and newer neighborhoods draw Denver and Boulder commuters.
  • Carbon Valley CO new homes — Firestone, Frederick, and Dacono anchor the entry-level new-construction curve.

The right city depends on where you work, where your kids go to school, and how much yard and house you want for the budget. Start with the SAA Homes buyer resource hub to browse local guides, or request a free home value report if you are weighing a move from one of these markets.
